Glossary

The canonical language for this course. Every lesson uses these terms; the "vs. LLM" note fixes each one against the machine intuition you already have.

Attention

Attention
The control of a limited processing resource by relevance — amplifying some information and suppressing the rest. In humans this is a hard bottleneck; in an LLM it is soft weighting over information that all remains present.
vs. LLM: same "weight by relevance" idea, minus the scarcity.
Biased competition
The mechanism of human attention: stimuli compete for scarce neural representation and mutually suppress each other; a goal-driven bias signal raises the gain on the relevant one so it wins.
Avoid: focusing, concentrating, paying attention
Top-down bias (dorsal)
A voluntary, goal-driven signal — from the dorsal frontoparietal system — that tips the competition toward what you intend to attend to. The closest analogue to a Transformer's query.
Avoid: willpower, effort, endogenous attention (alias, acceptable)
Bottom-up interrupt (ventral)
An involuntary, stimulus-driven reorienting — the ventral system's "circuit breaker" — that seizes attention toward salient or unexpected events, even against your goals. Has no clean Transformer counterpart.
Avoid: distraction, exogenous attention (alias, acceptable)
Gain
The strength/responsiveness applied to a neural representation. "Biasing the competition" physically means turning gain up on the winner and down on the losers.
Avoid: volume, attention level
