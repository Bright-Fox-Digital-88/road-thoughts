Attention as Selection

The word you already know from Transformers — and the one thing it quietly drops on the way into a human head.

You know what "attention" means inside an LLM: for every position, a query is compared against the keys of every other token, a softmax turns those comparisons into weights, and the output is a weighted blend of the values. It is relevance-weighted routing. The whole context is available; the mechanism just decides what to weight.

Human attention starts from the same idea — relevance-weighted selection — and then adds one thing the machine version doesn't have: scarcity. That single difference is the payload of this lesson.

The mechanism: biased competition

The dominant account in neuroscience is biased competition 1. Stimuli in your environment are not passively co-represented. They compete for a limited pool of neural representation, and they actively suppress each other. Two objects falling in the same receptive field do not both get encoded at full strength — they fight, and one wins.

What tips the fight is a top-down bias signal: your current goal reaches down and increases the "gain" on whatever is relevant, so it wins the competition and the rest is suppressed. If the query in a Transformer is what am I looking for right now, the goal-driven bias signal is the brain's closest equivalent — except here, winning the competition means the losers are largely not processed at all.

Parallel that holds

Both systems are content-based selection driven by a goal. A current query/goal decides which information gets amplified. Attention, in both, is the control of limited resources by relevance rather than by fixed wiring.

Two sources of control: top-down and bottom-up

Where does the bias come from? Two anatomically separate systems 2:

The dorsal system (frontal eye fields, intraparietal sulcus) is the one that feels like you deciding: sustained, voluntary, goal-driven. The ventral system is a stimulus-driven interrupt — it seizes control when something salient appears, then hands back. There is no "you" in the loop; it fires whether you want it to or not. This bottom-up interrupt is the piece with no counterpart in a standard Transformer.

The one thing to keep

LLM attention borrowed the weighting idea and dropped the scarcity. For a Transformer, attention is soft routing over information that is all still there. For a human, attention is a filter under a hard capacity limit — to attend to one thing is to not process the rest. When you catch yourself importing the LLM intuition, that's the assumption to check.

Check yourself

In biased competition, what does a top-down goal signal actually do?

Right: the goal biases an existing competition — it boosts the gain on relevant stimuli so they win and suppress the rest. It does not add capacity; the scarcity is the whole point.

Which feature of human attention has no clean Transformer counterpart?

The ventral, stimulus-driven interrupt — reorienting you against your goals whether you want it or not — is the piece a standard Transformer lacks. The other three are exactly where the analogy holds.
