Transformers & Attention · Lesson 6 of N

The Host's Stewards

Multi-head attention — the Host was never one reader, but a council. And one pair of stewards explains why prompting works at all.

The one idea: A single attention can form only one notion of relevance at a time. Real understanding needs several at once — so the Host is split into parallel stewards (heads), each reading the room through a different kind of resonance, their dance cards merged at the end. Some of these stewards are the mechanism behind in-context learning.

This lesson corrects the one simplification the Evening made: that the Host inks a single dance card. He never did. Seeing why completes the attention picture — no simplifications left.

Movement I · Why one reader was never enough

Your token often needs to attend to different things for different reasons at the same moment: the subject (to get agreement right), the adjective (for meaning), the noun a pronoun refers to (for sense). A single dance card can only express one distribution of attention — force all those jobs onto it and they smear together and interfere.

👥 The Council of Stewards the attention heads So the Host doesn't read the room alone. He sends several stewards — 8, 12, dozens — in parallel. Each carries its own Seeking, Calling, and Gift cards (its own Q/K/V projections), and each inks its own dance card. One steward attends to grammatical subjects; another to the previous token; another to a pronoun's referent; another to tone. Same room, several different kinds of resonance, read at once.
The elegant part: it's nearly free. The token's numbers are divided among the stewards — with 12 heads, each works in a slice 1/12 as wide — so all of them together cost about the same as one full-width reader. You buy diversity by partitioning, not by adding bulk.
Movement II · The stewards are real, named, and caught in the act

These aren't hypothetical. Interpretability researchers have reverse-engineered specific stewards in real models and given them names:

  • Previous-token stewards — attend to the token just before, copying its signal forward.
  • Syntactic stewards — link subjects to verbs, determiners to their nouns.
  • Name-mover stewards — carry the right name to where it's needed (found inside the famous "indirect object" circuit).
🔁 The Two Stewards Who Learn On The Fly induction heads → in-context learning The crown jewel. A pair of stewards works together: one notes "this token appeared before — here's what followed it last time"; the other says "so predict that again." In effect: find the earlier occurrence of the current pattern and copy what came next. This is the discovered mechanism behind in-context learning — why a model picks up your format from two examples, completes a pattern it was never trained on, and "just gets it" from the prompt. No weights change. Two stewards, reading the room, doing pattern-completion in real time.
This is the highest-leverage idea in the course for your work. When you write few-shot examples and the model follows the pattern — that's induction heads. When an agent picks up a tool-call format from one demonstration — induction heads. "In-context learning" isn't a vague talent; it's a specific, named mechanism a couple of attention heads perform. Prompt design is, in large part, giving these stewards a pattern clear enough to copy.
Movement III · The chamberlain merges the cards
🎩 The Chamberlain concatenation + the output projection Each steward pours its own small cup from its own dance card. The chamberlain gathers all those cups side by side (concatenation) and runs them through one final mixing (a learned output projection) to produce the single blend you actually drink — back at full width, ready to be added into your Ledger. Many parallel readings in; one merged update out.
So the full shape of one attention step: split into H stewards → each does the score-scale-softmax-blend you already know, in its own slice → concatenate → project → add to the residual stream. Everything from Lessons 1–3 was one steward's view; this wraps the council around it.
Movement IV · The state-view

Now that you're watching the whole board rather than a single guest: each layer is a council per position, all positions in parallel, all stewards in parallel — an enormous fan of simultaneous readings, every one of them just score-soften-blend on a slice of numbers. The Host's apparent single wisdom is really a crowd of narrow specialists whose votes are merged. The depth (layers) stacks these councils; the width (heads) diversifies each one.

The gears beneath this lesson

  • The Council of Stewards → attention heads: H parallel attentions per layer, each with its own Q/K/V in a 1/H-width slice; near-free because the dimensions are partitioned.
  • Named stewards → discovered specialized heads (previous-token, syntactic, name-mover).
  • The Two Stewards Who Learn On The Fly → induction heads: match the current pattern to an earlier occurrence and copy what followed — the mechanism of in-context learning.
  • The Chamberlain → concatenate the heads' outputs and apply a learned output projection to produce the single merged update.
