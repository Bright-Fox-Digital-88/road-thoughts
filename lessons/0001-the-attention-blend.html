The Attention Blend

What softmax actually does — turning Q·K scores into a weighted mix of values.

You left off at exactly the right cliff edge: you have your query, key, and value vectors, and you're staring at the softmax. This lesson crosses that gap. By the end you'll be able to say, precisely, what attention computes — not as a metaphor, but as an operation.

The one idea: Attention produces, for each token, a weighted average of every token's value vector. The weights say "how much should I listen to you?", and softmax is what turns raw compatibility scores into those weights.

The setup you already have

For every token, three learned projections produce three vectors (Illustrated Transformer):

  • query — "what am I looking for?"
  • key — "what do I offer, so others can find me?"
  • value — "the actual content I'll hand over if attended to."

The query/key split is the subtle part. A token doesn't ask "who is similar to me?" — it asks "who is relevant to me?" The word it isn't similar to cat in embedding space, yet cat is exactly what it needs. Separate Q and K projections let the model learn relevance instead of settling for raw similarity.

The recipe: scaled dot-product attention

This is the whole mechanism, in four moves (paper §3.2, Vaswani et al. 2017):

  1. Score. Take one token's query and dot it with every token's key. A big dot product = "you're relevant to me." This gives one raw score per token.
  2. Scale. Divide every score by √dk (the key dimension). Without this, large dimensions make the dot products huge, and softmax would saturate into a near-one-hot spike with vanishing gradients. The scale keeps it sane.
  3. Softmax. Push the scaled scores through softmax. Now they're all positive and sum to 1 — a probability distribution over "how much attention to pay to each token."
  4. Blend. Multiply each token's value by its weight and add them all up. That weighted sum is this token's attention output.

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q·Kᵀ / √dk ) · V

That single line is the entire heart of "Attention Is All You Need." Everything else — multi-head, stacking, feed-forward layers — wraps around this.

A worked blend

Say the token it produces three scaled scores against cat, mat, and sat:

againstscaled score (Q·K)after softmax (weight)
cat4.00.84
mat2.00.11
sat1.00.05

Softmax exponentiates each score and normalizes, so the gap between 4.0 and 2.0 becomes a large gap in weight (0.84 vs 0.11) — exponentials exaggerate. The output for it is then:

0.84 · Vcat + 0.11 · Vmat + 0.05 · Vsat

Mostly cat's value, a whisper of the others. The token it has just pulled in the meaning of cat — that's coreference resolution falling out of pure arithmetic.

Notice nothing here is "fetched" or "stored" in the database sense. Attention never picks one winner; it always returns a blend. Even a confident 0.84 still mixes in the rest. That softness is exactly why it's differentiable and trainable.

Why divide the scores by √dk before softmax?
When you stuff more context into a prompt, every token's query now scores against every other token's key — an N×N comparison. That quadratic blow-up is why long context is slow and expensive, and why the attention weights are where "what the model is paying attention to" literally lives. You're now looking at the exact operation your agents pay for, token by token.
