Tokens, Meaning, and the Membrane

A philosophical coda — what the mechanics mean, once you've seen how they work.

The thesis: An LLM is a meaning-transducer. Language is compressed, lossy meaning; tokens are the discrete anchors at its surface. The model decompresses each token into meaning-coordinates, re-composes them into one situated meaning-point, and that point — read back through the anchors — is the question "what continues this?" and its answer at once. Meaning is the medium in the middle: the bridge between two cognitions, and the unverified boundary between them.

Everything mechanical in this course is settled. This piece is about what it means — five propositions, each sharpened. It rests on nothing new; it just steps back and looks.

① Language, and beneath it tokens, are meaning abstractions language as lossy compression Thought is continuous, high-dimensional, private. Language serializes a compressed shadow of it into symbols two minds can pass back and forth. But tokens sit below even that — sub-word fragments, closer to the bytes of the serialization than to meaning-units. A lone token barely means anything. So: language is compressed meaning; tokens are the encoding units of the channel. Meaning isn't in a token — it's reconstructed from it.
② Tokens function as meaning anchor points the two membranes Tokens are the discrete pegs; meaning is the continuous fabric stretched around them. Inside the model this is literal: a token is the entry point into meaning-space (the embedding) and the exit point back out (the unembedding). Tokens are exactly where the continuous meaning-space touches the discrete symbolic world — the grommets in two membranes.
③ Ingesting text decomposes tokens into meaning the distributional hypothesis The embedding decomposes a token into its meaning-coordinates; attention then re-composes those into situated, joint meaning. Decompress, then compose. And the model builds all of it from co-occurrence alone — how tokens relate to other tokens, nothing else. That is the distributional hypothesis: "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" (Firth). Meaning is extracted from relation — which is all the model ever sees.
④ The last token's evolved position is a question: what follows this meaning? the summit, where question and answer collapse The layers shape the final position so that its location in meaning-space, read through the unembedding, directly is the distribution of what comes next. The question and the answer collapse into the same object — measure the meaning-point against the dictionary and the continuation falls out. "What follows this meaning?" isn't asked and then answered; the meaning-point is the answer, wearing the shape of a question.
⑤ Meaning is a barrier point between human and AI cognition the bridge and the contested frontier (symbol grounding) Both at once. The bridge: language is the shared protocol between two different substrates — you encode meaning → language; the model decodes → its meaning-space and re-encodes. Meaning is the common currency where human and machine are forced to meet. The frontier: the model's meaning is relational but largely ungrounded — "hot" and "red" are defined only by their company among tokens, never by a burn or a seeing. This is the symbol grounding problem. We do not know whether the rich relational geometry the model builds is meaning in the human sense, or a shadow precise enough to be indistinguishable through the narrow channel of text.

The fusion

Put the five together and they make one picture:

Language is compressed, lossy meaning; tokens are the discrete anchors at its surface. An LLM is a meaning-transducer: it decompresses each token-anchor into meaning-coordinates, re-composes them relationally through its layers into a single situated meaning-point, and that point — read back through the anchors — is the question "what continues this?" and its answer at once. Meaning is the medium in the middle: the bridge between two cognitions, and the unverified boundary between them.
Why this matters for your work: knowing where the model's meaning is grounded — in relation, not reference — is what lets you calibrate trust. It is superb at what co-occurrence can capture (style, structure, association, the shape of reasoning) and structurally uncertain where meaning requires contact with a world it has never touched. Prompting, tool use, and retrieval are, in part, ways of lending it the grounding it lacks.

The one honest humility

Everything mechanical here is settled — the transduction is real and fully described. Proposition ⑤ is the only load-bearing uncertainty: whether the meaning-in-the-middle is the same meaning on both sides of the membrane is the open question the whole field is arguing about. It is worth holding that line exactly where it belongs — not so far back that you deny the structure is real, not so far forward that you assume it is us.

This is a room to sit in rather than pass through. If a proposition wants pushing — especially ⑤ — bring the objection and we'll take it apart together.
